Foundations

Analysis of complex spatial data

Spatial data is far more than imagery. It flows from field surveys and sensor networks, cadastral and administrative registries, statistical series, community mapping, and Earth observation. The programme trains the methods that bring these sources together and turn them into measurement — at the scale where questions become answers.

01

Sources of spatial data

Vector layers describe cadastral parcels, administrative boundaries, and infrastructure networks; field surveys and GNSS return precise measurements; sensor networks and moving-object trajectories add position through time; censuses and official registries bring socio-economic depth; community mapping contributes open, current detail. Earth observation — optical and radar satellites, and airborne LiDAR — is one powerful family among many.

02

Spatial analysis

Analysis is where data becomes insight. Spatial statistics measure autocorrelation and reveal clusters and hotspots; interpolation and geostatistics estimate continuous surfaces from scattered samples; overlay and suitability modelling weigh many criteria at once; network analysis finds shortest paths and service areas; change detection tracks how places evolve through time.

03

Multi-source integration

Real questions rarely fit one dataset. Bringing field measurements, vector layers, elevation, and imagery into a common coordinate frame — and reasoning about their differing resolution, accuracy, and uncertainty — is the analytical core of the discipline: heterogeneous sources resolved into a single, defensible answer.
